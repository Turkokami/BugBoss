## Nine times out of ten it is not what you think

Somebody finds a dark, fast, inch-and-a-half insect in the basement in Waupaca County and the word that comes to mind is *cockroach*. The next thing that happens is usually a trip to the hardware store for a fogger, which is the worst possible response to an actual German cockroach and a completely unnecessary one for what they actually found.

So it is worth taking the identification seriously, because there are really two different conversations here.

**Conversation one:** you have an outdoor insect that wandered in. This is most cases in rural Northeast Wisconsin. It is a nuisance and a moisture indicator, and the fix is a dehumidifier, a downspout, and a door sweep.

**Conversation two:** you have German cockroaches. They breed indoors, they compound quickly, they carry a real allergen burden, and the response has to be right the first time or the problem gets bigger and moves.

Telling them apart takes about thirty seconds.

## The one that matters: the German cockroach

Look for three things together.

**Small.** Roughly half an inch. Noticeably smaller than most of the things people bring us in a jar.

**Light tan to brown**, not dark and glossy.

**Two dark parallel stripes** running front-to-back on the shield behind the head. This is the diagnostic mark and it is visible in a phone photo.

And then the behavior, which is as good a clue as the appearance:

- **Kitchens and bathrooms**, not basements. They want warmth, moisture, and food together.
- **Around and inside appliances** — the motor housing of the fridge, under and behind the dishwasher, inside the coffee maker, the microwave, the toaster oven. Warm electronics are prime real estate.
- **They run for a crack the instant the light comes on.**
- **Seeing them in daylight is a bad sign**, not a neutral one. German cockroaches are nocturnal and stay hidden when the population is small. Daytime activity generally means the harborage is crowded, which means the population is well past the early stage.

They also leave signs before you see the insects: **small dark specks like ground pepper** in drawers, in cabinet corners, along the underside of a countertop lip, and around hinges; **cast skins**; and in a heavy population, a distinctive musty smell.

## The ones that mostly do not matter

**Wood cockroaches.** The common rural false alarm. These live outdoors under bark, in woodpiles, in leaf litter, and in outbuildings. Males fly, they come to lights, and in late spring and early summer they blunder into houses in numbers that alarm people. They are similar in size to a German cockroach and can even show a pale edge that people mistake for stripes.

The thing to know: **they cannot establish indoors.** There is nothing in your house for them and they die off. If you are finding them near the woodpile, the door, the porch light, or the outbuildings — and finding them in June — you almost certainly have wood cockroaches, and the answer is moving the woodpile away from the house, sealing the entry gaps, and adjusting exterior lighting.

**Oriental cockroaches.** Bigger, dark, glossy, slow-moving, and found in cool damp places — floor drains, crawlspaces, basements, sump areas, and around water heaters. Sometimes called water bugs. These are a genuine pest, but the driver is moisture and drainage, and the fix leans heavily on drying things out, sealing drains, and exclusion rather than on ongoing treatment.

**Ground beetles and crickets.** Both are frequently reported as roaches. Ground beetles are hard-shelled, dark, often with visible wing covers meeting in a straight line down the back, and they wander in under doors. [Crickets](/pest-library/crickets/) — including camel crickets in damp basements — get called roaches constantly.

If what you found does not have the two stripes and was not in the kitchen, the odds are strongly in your favor. Send a photo and find out before you buy anything.

## Why German cockroaches get out of hand so fast

The reason a German cockroach problem needs a fast response is arithmetic.

A female carries her egg case with her until close to hatching — which is itself part of why they are hard to control, since the eggs are protected inside a hiding place rather than left in the open. Each case contains dozens of eggs, she produces several over her life, and in a warm building the generation time is weeks, not seasons.

The practical consequence is that **the cost of the job is mostly determined by how long it ran before anyone called.** A dozen roaches in January is a straightforward job. The same population left until April has spread from the kitchen to the bathroom, into the wall voids, and in an apartment building, into the units on either side.

This is also why "I killed the ones I saw and they came back" is the universal experience. The ones you saw were the overflow.

## The fogger problem, stated plainly

The single most common mistake — and the one that reliably makes things worse — is the **total-release fogger**, the bug bomb.

Here is what actually happens when one goes off in a kitchen with German cockroaches in it:

1. The fog settles on **open horizontal surfaces**. The roaches are not on open horizontal surfaces. They are inside cracks, voids, appliance housings, hollow table legs, and the space behind the cabinet kick plate, and the fog does not reach any of it.
2. The material is **repellent**, so roaches that detect it move **away** — deeper into the wall voids, into the adjacent room, and in a multi-unit building, into the neighbor's kitchen through the shared plumbing chase.
3. Now the population is **dispersed across a larger area**, in harborage that is harder to reach, and any subsequent treatment has more ground to cover.
4. Separately, foggers release a large volume of flammable propellant into an enclosed space with pilot lights and electrical contacts in it. House fires and explosions from foggers are a documented and entirely avoidable hazard.

Hardware-store aerosols sprayed along a baseboard have a milder version of the same problem: repellency and dispersal without penetration.

**What works instead is bait**, placed in the harborage — a non-repellent material the roaches feed on and carry back, which reaches the ones you never see. Sprays and baits actively work against each other, which is one of the practical reasons a DIY spray campaign before a professional visit makes the professional visit harder.

## The health side is real

Two things, and the first one gets underrated.

**Allergens.** Cockroach droppings, cast skins, and body fragments break down into fine particulate that becomes part of household dust, and cockroach allergen is a recognized trigger for asthma — with the strongest documented effect in children living in multi-unit housing. In a home with an asthmatic child, a roach problem is a health matter and not just an unpleasant one. We treat those calls accordingly.

**Mechanical contamination.** Cockroaches move between drains, garbage, and food-preparation surfaces, and they carry whatever is on their bodies with them. This is why a health inspector reacts the way they do to a roach finding in a commercial kitchen, and it is covered in the [Wisconsin food service requirements](/compliance/food-service-pest-requirements/) page.

## Where they come from, since it is usually not the housekeeping

German cockroaches do not appear spontaneously and they do not usually walk in from outdoors — they are not really an outdoor insect in this climate. They get **carried in**:

- **Grocery bags and cardboard boxes**, especially cardboard, which is both a hiding place and a food source.
- **Secondhand appliances and furniture.** Used microwaves, toaster ovens, and refrigerators are a classic vector; the motor housing is warm and enclosed.
- **Deliveries and shipments**, including into restaurants and stores.
- **Shared walls, plumbing chases, and hallways** in apartments, duplexes, and senior housing.
- **Luggage, bags, and boxes** moving between an infested building and a clean one.

Which means a spotless apartment next to an infested one can get roaches without anyone in it doing a single thing wrong. If you rent, the [Wisconsin landlord and tenant pest rules](/compliance/landlord-tenant-pest-control/) page covers how responsibility is generally handled and why reporting it in writing immediately is in your interest.

Where housekeeping does matter is after arrival. Grease film on and behind the range, crumbs under the toe kick, standing water in a sink overnight, pet food left down, cardboard stored under the sink — these are what let a small introduction become a large population. Sanitation does not decide whether you get them. It decides how fast they grow.

## What actually helps while you arrange treatment

- **Kill the water.** Fix the dripping trap and the running toilet, dry the sink at night, empty pet bowls overnight, and wipe up condensation. Roaches survive far longer without food than without water.
- **Get the grease.** Behind and under the range, under the toe kick, the sides of the stove, the range hood filter, the top of the fridge.
- **Lose the cardboard.** Move dry goods into sealed containers and get boxes out of the cupboards and out of the pantry. Corrugated cardboard is roach habitat with a food supply built in.
- **Vacuum the harborage**, then empty outdoors into sealed trash. This physically removes insects, egg cases, and allergen at once, and it is genuinely effective.
- **Do not spray**, for the reasons above. If you have already sprayed, say so when you call — it changes the approach.
- **Do not move furniture or appliances into another room or unit.**

## What a real cockroach job looks like

Inspection first, because the treatment follows the harborage: hinges and corners of cabinets, the motor compartments of appliances, under and behind the range and fridge, the dishwasher void, the sink base, cracked tile and grout, the toe kick, hollow legs and voids in commercial equipment, and drains. Monitors go in to establish where the population actually is rather than where the sightings happen to be.

Then a **bait-led program**, with the sanitation and exclusion work identified and communicated, and **follow-up visits** — because eggs hatch after the first treatment and something has to be waiting for them. One visit does not finish a German cockroach job, and anybody who says otherwise is selling.
