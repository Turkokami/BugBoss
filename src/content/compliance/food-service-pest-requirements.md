## The rule, stated plainly

**A Wisconsin retail food establishment must keep the premises free of insects, rodents, and other pests — and must control them by eliminating entry and harborage, using approved methods.**

Wisconsin's retail food requirements sit in **ATCP 75**, which adopts the Wisconsin Food Code. They are administered by **DATCP**, and in much of the state by **agent health departments** at county or municipal level — which is who is likely to actually walk through your door.

The important structural point: the rule is written around an **outcome**, not a schedule. It does not say "have a monthly contract." It says the premises shall be free of pests. That is why holding a contract is not, on its own, an answer to a live-activity finding — and why a well-run establishment with good exclusion sometimes needs less service than a poorly-sealed one needs.

## Who this applies to

Retail food establishments across Wisconsin — restaurants, taverns, cafés, bakeries, delis, grocery and convenience stores, school and institutional kitchens, mobile units, and temporary event food operations.

Requirements scale with the operation. A food processing plant carries a heavier regime — FSMA and 21 CFR 117 sit on top of state rules — covered on our [food processing](/commercial-pest-control/food-processing/) page. Dairy processing adds PMO requirements; see [dairy and cheese processing](/commercial-pest-control/dairy-cheese-processing/).

## The obligations

**Keep the premises free of pests.** The headline duty.

**Exclude them.** Tight-fitting exterior doors with intact sweeps, screened openings, sealed pipe and conduit penetrations, no gaps at the roofline or dock. This is the part that actually determines whether you have a recurring problem, and it is the part most often deferred.

**Remove harborage and food sources.** Cleaning under and behind equipment, managing spills and grease, controlling clutter and unused equipment, keeping storage off the floor and away from walls so inspection is possible.

**Manage the exterior.** Dumpster lids closed and containers sound, pad kept clean, adequate collection frequency, grease containers managed, standing water addressed. Most interior problems begin outside.

**Use approved control methods.** Product labels heavily restrict what may be used in food-handling areas, and many products are simply not permitted there. This is where staff-applied retail sprays cause trouble — see [applicator licensing](/compliance/pesticide-applicator-licensing/).

**Be able to show it.** Service records, monitoring logs, and evidence of corrective action.

## What an inspector actually looks for

In roughly this order:

**Live activity.** Anything moving. The most serious finding.

**Evidence.** Rodent droppings along walls and behind equipment, gnaw marks, urine staining under UV, insect casings and shed skins, dead insects accumulating in light fixtures and window channels. Inspectors look behind and underneath things, because that is where evidence survives cleaning.

**Entry points.** Daylight under the back door is the classic. Gaps around pipes, unscreened vents, damaged door sweeps, floor drains without covers.

**Harborage.** Clutter, unused equipment, cardboard stored on the floor, voids nobody cleans.

**Sources.** Standing water, drain biofilm, grease accumulation, spills under equipment, the mop sink area.

**The dumpster and dock.** Frequently the origin of everything else.

**Your records.** Produced promptly, current, and showing that previous findings were acted on.

## What happens if it is ignored

**Written violations** with correction deadlines and a follow-up inspection — by far the most common outcome.

**Escalation** for repeat or uncorrected findings.

**Closure**, where conditions present an imminent health hazard. Possible, and a severe active infestation can reach that threshold.

**Public record.** Inspection results are generally public, and in a small Wisconsin community a rodent finding circulates fast. For most operators this is the consequence that actually hurts.

**Product loss and contamination**, which carries its own cost before any regulator is involved.
