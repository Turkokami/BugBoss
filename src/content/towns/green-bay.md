## The specific thing most Green Bay pest companies will not do

Call around about a raccoon in a wall and you will find the pattern quickly. A lot of pest control companies serving the metro will treat insects and rodents and stop there. Wildlife gets referred out — to a trapper who will remove the animal and leave, or to nobody at all.

The problem with removal-and-leave is that **the animal was never the durable part of the problem.** The torn soffit it came through is still open next week. So is the gap at the fascia that the next one will use, and the entry the [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/) will find in October, and the same joint the [wasps](/residential-pest-control/stinging-insects/) will build in next July.

What we do instead runs the full sequence: find every entry point from the outside, get the animals out with one-way devices rather than guesswork, confirm the space is actually empty, seal it with hardware cloth and flashing that is mechanically fastened rather than foam in a hole, and clean up what got left behind — the latrine, the guano, the insulation that has lost its R-value and its hygiene at the same time.

That is described properly on the [wildlife removal](/wildlife-removal/) page and in the [attic wildlife guide](/guides/attic-wildlife-removal/).

## The building stock is the whole story here

Green Bay is not one pest environment. It is at least three, and which one you are in determines what you are dealing with.

**The century-old near-downtown and near-east neighborhoods.** Balloon framing, settled trim, original soffit runs, and generous attics. In a balloon-framed wall the cavity runs open from the sill plate to the attic, so an insect or a mouse that enters at the foundation has an uninterrupted route to a second-floor bedroom. This is why [cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) turn up at an upstairs window in February in a house where nothing visibly gets in downstairs, and it is why exclusion in these homes has to address the roofline and the foundation as one system rather than two. The [Astor neighborhood page](/pest-control/green-bay-astor/) goes into this.

**The postwar east-side grid.** Ranches and capes on smaller lots, generally tighter than the old stock but with their own recurring weak points — attached garages that were never sealed at the slab, soffit vents with failed screening, and utility penetrations nobody closed. Out toward the Baird Creek greenway the field-and-woodland edge pushes [mice](/pest-library/mice/), [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/), and fall invaders straight into the neighborhood. The [Preble page](/pest-control/green-bay-preble/) covers that side.

**The newer subdivisions on the edge.** Built on what was recently farmland, which is exactly the setup that produces heavy first-few-years rodent pressure and cluster flies off the surrounding soil. New construction is not immune here; it is often worse for the first three or four autumns, because the field population has not yet adjusted to the buildings and nobody sealed the penetrations when the house went up.

## Water is the other constant

The bay, the Fox River, the East River, and the creek corridors run right through the city, and moisture drives a whole category of calls that people misread as an insect problem.

[Springtails](/pest-library/springtails/), [millipedes](/pest-library/millipedes/), [sowbugs](/pest-library/sowbugs-pillbugs/), and [centipedes](/pest-library/centipedes/) in a basement are moisture indicators before they are anything else, and the honest answer is often a dehumidifier, a downspout extension, and a regraded flowerbed rather than a treatment program. We will tell you that. Standing water in the corridors and in yards also drives [mosquito](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/) pressure through the summer, and the greenways carry the [tick](/pest-library/ticks/) risk that people associate with the country and then encounter half a mile from a grocery store.

## Multi-unit and commercial work

A shared structure is one building, and treating it as several is the single most common reason a bed bug or cockroach problem in a Green Bay apartment building runs for years.

[Bed bugs](/pest-library/bed-bugs/) and [cockroaches](/pest-library/cockroaches/) move through plumbing chases, shared walls, and hallways. Treating unit 3 while units 2 and 4 go untreated means the population relocates and comes back, and everyone concludes that treatment does not work. It does; the scope was wrong. We have taken on an [eight-unit building with a multi-year infestation and brought it to zero](/case-studies/eight-plex-bed-bug-elimination/), and the thing that made it work was treating it as one job.
