faqs:
  - q: "How does a nest get that big without anyone noticing?"
    a: "Because an attic is exactly the right kind of space: undisturbed, sheltered, and rarely entered. A yellowjacket colony founded by a single queen in spring grows all season, and inside a protected void there is nothing to check it — no weather, no predators, no disturbance. Nobody opens the attic hatch between March and September, so the first sign is often the sound, or wasps appearing at a ceiling light fixture, by which point the colony has had months to build."
  - q: "Is a nest that size dangerous?"
    a: "Yes, and it is genuinely not a homeowner job. Yellowjackets defend a nest aggressively, they can sting repeatedly, and a colony of that size represents a very large number of insects in an enclosed space with one way out. Disturbing it — including by spraying into it from a hatch and retreating — provokes exactly the mass response you do not want in a confined attic. Anyone with a sting allergy in the household changes the risk from serious to medical emergency."
  - q: "Why not just spray it from the entrance?"
    a: "Because a nest in a void has an inside and an outside, and treating the visible entrance often just drives the colony deeper or opens a new exit into the living space below. That is the classic DIY outcome with wall-void and attic nests: the wasps that were confined to the attic start appearing in bedrooms. The nest has to be dealt with as a whole, and on a colony that size that means the right equipment, the right protection, and a plan for getting out."
  - q: "Does the nest have to be removed as well as killed?"
    a: "For one that size, yes. A large abandoned nest in an attic is a substantial mass of paper material that holds dead insects and larvae, and it attracts secondary pests that feed on the remains. It can also cause staining below. Small exterior nests can often be left to weather away; an eight-foot nest inside a structure is a removal job, not just an elimination one."
  - q: "Will they rebuild in the same attic?"
    a: "Yellowjacket colonies do not overwinter and reuse a nest the way some animals reuse a den — the colony dies off and only new queens survive. But the attic that suited one colony suits the next, and the entry point that let them in is still there. So the durable fix is sealing the way in, which is the part worth doing once the nest is out."
