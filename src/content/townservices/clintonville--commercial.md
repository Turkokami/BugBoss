## Being in the same village changes the service, not just the drive

Most of the commercial pest work in Clintonville is not technically difficult. What makes it work or not work is whether somebody can be at the back door of a restaurant at six in the morning, know where the drains are, and notice that the dock seal has gone since last month.

That is a proximity problem, and we are at **600 S. Main St.** It is also a continuity problem: on a commercial account, the person who already knows your building is worth considerably more than the person who knows the product catalog. Around here that person is Brian, not a route technician who has never been in the place.

## What you actually get in writing

**Every visit produces a work order** — what was inspected, what was found, what was treated and when. That is the record a health inspector or a sanitarian normally asks to see, and keeping it current is the point of it.

If your building answers to more than that — a third-party audit scheme, a franchise brand standard, a customer QA requirement — those schemes typically want a defined package on top: a written scope, device maps, a trend log, corrective-action records, product labels and safety data sheets, and proof of licensing and insurance. **Tell us what yours requires before the program starts** so we can agree in writing who maintains which piece. Discovering that gap during an audit is the outcome worth avoiding, and it is avoidable in one conversation at the outset.

Licensing and insurance are straightforward: Brian Zahringer holds Wisconsin Commercial Applicator certification #103689 in the aquatic and mosquito and structural categories, and a certificate of insurance is issued on request.

## The seasonal shape of it

Commercial buildings in this village run the same calendar as the houses, with sharper consequences.

- **Late August into September** — the fall-invader window. On a commercial building this matters most for anywhere the public sees the walls and windows, and for food premises where a mass of insects at a window is a conversation with an inspector.
- **October and November** — [rodents](/pest-library/mice/) moving in off the fields. Docks, overhead doors and the gap under metal skirting are the entries.
- **Through the summer** — [flies](/pest-library/flies/) and drain flies in kitchens, [wasps](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) on eaves and near entrances and dumpsters, and [mosquitoes](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/) around patios and outdoor seating.
- **Year-round** — [cockroaches](/pest-library/cockroaches/) and [bed bugs](/pest-library/bed-bugs/), which do not have a season and get worse the longer the scope is wrong.

## What we will tell you not to buy

A lot of commercial pest control is sold as a monthly visit that mostly consists of somebody walking through with a sprayer. If your building's problem is a dock seal, a drain that never gets scrubbed, or a bulk-storage habit, a recurring treatment manages the symptom indefinitely and never ends it.

We would rather identify that and tell you, even where the honest recommendation is a repair and a change of practice rather than a service agreement. That is easier to do when there is no contract on the table, which is part of why there isn't one.
