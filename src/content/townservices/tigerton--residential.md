## Fields on one side, woods on the other

Tigerton sits on the **Embarrass River** in Shawano County, ringed by **farmland and woodlots**. That combination is the whole story for pest work here, because it means the property line is where two different pest environments meet.

The open farmland side delivers **rodents and fall invaders** — the animals and insects that live out in the fields and lawns all summer and come looking for shelter as soon as the nights drop. The woodlot side adds **ticks, carpenter ants and wildlife**. A village home with a field behind it and trees along the drive gets both.

What almost nothing here is caused by: housekeeping. That is worth saying plainly, because it is the thing people quietly worry about, and the two dominant pests in this village have nothing whatever to do with the state of your kitchen.

## The two events that define the year

**Mid-September into October — fall invaders.**

[Cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/), [boxelder bugs](/pest-library/boxelder-bugs/) and [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) mass on warm south and west walls and work into every seam they can find. Older farmhouses get a much harder version of this than newer homes, for reasons that are purely structural:

- A **tall, pale house standing alone in open country** is the most conspicuous warm surface for a mile.
- **Balloon framing** in pre-1940 houses leaves wall cavities running open from sill plate to attic, so anything entering at the foundation has an uninterrupted route to the second floor.
- **A century of settlement** has opened joints, trim and masonry in ways routine maintenance never fully closes.

The fix is timed rather than reactive: an **exterior treatment in late August or early September**, before the insects arrive on the siding, plus sealing the entries. The same treatment in late October, once they are in the wall, does very little — and that gap is the single most common reason people conclude treatment does not work.

**October into December — rodents.**

The fields get harvested, the nights drop, and [mice](/pest-library/mice/) move toward the buildings. A quarter inch is enough. Every frost-heaved foundation joint, every unsealed utility penetration and every gap under metal skirting is a door.

## Why trapping alone never finishes it

Catching mice indoors removes the ones you caught. It does nothing about the population outside producing them, which around Tigerton is effectively unlimited.

Three things actually change the outcome:

1. **Seal the house** — foundation line, sill plate, utility penetrations, window wells, door sweeps, and the roofline where last winter's ice dams did their work.
2. **Deal with the outbuildings** — the [pole barn](/blog/mice-in-the-pole-barn/), machine shed, granary and coop are reservoirs, and the skirting is usually where they get in.
3. **Manage the feed** — anything in a paper or plastic bag has been fed on. Metal cans with tight lids, spillage cleaned up.

The [farmhouse rodent exclusion guide](/guides/farmhouse-rodent-exclusion/) is the long version.

## What to do in the middle of winter

Nothing much, and that is the honest answer. Once the fall invaders are in the wall voids, no treatment reaches them and sealing traps them inside — where they die, and where dead insects in a void draw [carpet beetles](/pest-library/carpet-beetles/) later.

So: **vacuum rather than crush** (Asian lady beetles stain, stink bugs smell), and use the winter to **make the list** of where they are appearing — which rooms, which windows, which side of the house, ceiling or window frame. That list is next August's work order, and having it in hand in summer is what turns intending to fix it into fixing it. [The full explanation](/blog/bugs-indoors-in-january/).

## The woodlot side

**[Ticks](/pest-library/ticks/)** — the genuine health risk here, concentrated at the edges: where mowed lawn meets woods, along fencerows, in brush and rock piles. A daily tick check with same-day removal is the highest-value habit available, because Lyme transmission generally needs a long attachment. [The yard program](/guides/tick-lyme-yard-program/).

**[Carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ants/)** — wherever farmhouse wood has been wet. They excavate rather than eat wood, so finding them is usually how people discover a gutter, grade or flashing problem. Large black ants active indoors in February or March generally mean a nest inside the structure, since outdoor colonies are still dormant.

**[Wasps and hornets](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/)** — July through September, and around here that means outbuildings as much as eaves. Nests in machine sheds and granaries grow large precisely because nobody is in there weekly to notice, and ground nests along field edges are what catch people mowing. [Which nests can wait](/blog/wasp-hornet-nest-when-to-call/).

On a rural property the two weeks after the snow comes off are the most revealing of the year — the ground is bare, nothing has grown back, and every vole runway, frost gap and ice-damaged soffit is visible from the driveway. [The spring walk-around](/blog/spring-thaw-pest-check/), in order.
