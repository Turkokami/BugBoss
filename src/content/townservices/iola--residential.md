## The defining fact here is that nobody is home

Iola sits in the Chain O' Lakes country, and a large share of the buildings we work on are **cabins, cottages and seasonal homes** — occupied for a few months and closed for the rest.

That changes pest control more than people expect, in one specific way: **there is nobody there to notice a problem starting.** In a year-round house you see the first mouse dropping, the first wasp at the eave, the first fly at the window, and you act. In a cabin, the first thing you see is the accumulated result of eight months, discovered all at once in May.

So the work has a different rhythm. It is **timed around opening and closing** rather than spread across a quarterly schedule, and the highest-value visit of the year is the one that happens as you shut the place up.

## Two visits, and what each one is for

**Closing, in fall.** This is the one that matters. Late September or early October, after the [fall invaders](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) have been treated on the exterior and before the building is shut. The point is that the building is **sealed and left in a state that will not draw anything** — because whatever gets in during October has until May to make itself at home.

**Opening, in spring.** Deal with whatever got in anyway, and inspect what the winter did: frost movement at the piers or slab, snow-load damage at the roof edge, screens and vents that failed unwatched, and the [wasp queens](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) that are looking for a nest site right now while the nests are still the size of a walnut.

A quarterly program on a building nobody occupies is mostly wasted money, and we would rather say so. Our [cabin and seasonal pest control guide](/guides/cabin-seasonal-pest-control/) and the [closing checklist](/blog/cabin-closing-pest-checklist/) cover the method in full.

## What you can do yourself at closing

Genuinely most of it, and it costs nothing:

- **Strip the food out**, including what people forget — pantry staples, spices, coffee, pet food, birdseed in the shed, and candles and soap, which rodents chew.
- **Anything staying goes in metal or hard plastic** with a tight lid. Not cardboard, not a plastic bag.
- **Strip the beds**, and store bedding and cushions off the floor in sealed bins.
- **Leave interior doors and cupboards open** so nothing gets a dark undisturbed cavity to nest in.
- **Deal with the drains** — pull the traps or add antifreeze so they do not dry out and let sewer flies through.
- **Walk the outside** and note every gap. Then get them sealed before you leave, not after you find out.

## The pests that define this area

**[Mice](/pest-library/mice/)** — the number one seasonal-property problem, every year, for the reason above. A quarter inch is enough.

**[Cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/)** — a cabin sitting alone in open or wooded ground is exactly the conspicuous warm surface they aim for, and an unoccupied building means nobody vacuums them for eight months.

**[Bats](/pest-library/bats/)** — older cottages with open soffits and unscreened gable vents are ideal roosts, and an undisturbed building gets found. This one has a legal calendar and it collides awkwardly with the season you are actually there. See [the bat exclusion law](/guides/wisconsin-bat-exclusion-law/).

**[Wasps and hornets](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/)** — seasonal structures grow the worst nests precisely because nobody disturbs them from May to August. [Which ones can wait and which cannot](/blog/wasp-hornet-nest-when-to-call/).

**[Ticks](/pest-library/ticks/)** — the genuine health risk on a wooded lot, and it lives at the edges rather than in the middle of the mowed area. Woodland edge, tall grass along the drive, brush and rock piles, under the deck. A daily tick check with same-day removal is the highest-value habit there is. [The yard program](/guides/tick-lyme-yard-program/).

**[Mosquitoes](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/)** — lake country, so source reduction on your own property matters more than anything sprayed: the tarp, the tire, the boat cover, the clogged gutter, the saucer under the planter.
