## The reason we drive down here

Most pest control companies working the Fox Valley — including the franchises — **do not do wildlife at all.** They treat insects and rodents, and when there is an animal in a wall they refer it out to somebody else, or to nobody.

That is not a criticism. Wildlife work is a different trade: it is carpentry and animal handling as much as it is pest control, and it does not fit a route schedule.

Out in the rural corridor there is frequently nobody else to hand a raccoon to, so BugBoss learned to do the animal work and the building work together. In a metro that combination turns out to be comparatively rare — and it is the one job where thirty minutes in the truck is clearly worth it for both of us.

## Removal is the easy part. The building is the job.

Nearly every wildlife call we take in Appleton is booked as an animal problem and turns out to be a carpentry problem wearing an animal's face.

Remove the raccoon and the opening it tore stays open. That same gap is what the [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/) coming off the ravine trees will find in October, what the [wasps](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) will build into next July, and the way every [cluster fly](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [lady beetle](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) on the block gets in come September. The animal changes; the hole does not.

One repair closes all of it. Which is why what we sell is not really removal — it is **getting the animals out, closing the building, and cleaning up what got left behind.**

## Telling them apart before anyone climbs

The **time of day** does most of the identification:

- **Dawn and dusk, scratching and a rolling sound, silence overnight** — [gray squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/). Strictly daytime animals, and the rolling is an acorn being moved across the joists.
- **After dark, heavy and deliberate, with dragging** — [raccoons](/pest-library/raccoons/). If it sounds like a person is up there, it probably is one of these. The most destructive of the group and the one most likely to injure someone; do not go up.
- **Fast, light, constant, at night, in walls as well as overhead** — [mice](/pest-library/mice/).
- **Squeaking and a dry rustle at dusk, from a wall or soffit, with staining at an exterior gap** — [bats](/pest-library/bats/).
- **A smell and no noise at all** — something died in a void. Blow flies in the living space are usually how people find out.

The longer version: [reading the noise before you call anyone](/blog/squirrels-raccoons-in-the-attic/).

## The ravines put squirrels on your roof

This is the Appleton-specific part.

The city's ravine and creek corridors carry mature tree cover right up to the back of residential lots, which gives squirrels a continuous highway onto rooflines across the city. A limb within about **ten feet of the roof** is a bridge. So is a power line landing on the building, and a fence rail running to the garage.

Squirrels gnaw wiring — a genuine fire risk — and they open soffit and fascia getting in. The job is one-way exclusion, then repair, then cutting the access back. Trapping empties the attic; only the sealing keeps it empty.

## Bats: the calendar is not optional

Bats are protected in Wisconsin, and exclusion is off the table through the summer maternity season while flightless pups are still in the roost. Close a roost in that window and the pups die inside your ceiling. That is illegal, it is inhumane, and from a purely practical angle it leaves you with a far worse problem than the colony ever was.

So a bat call in June gets an inspection, a full plan, and a booked date rather than immediate work. We would rather tell you that and lose the momentum than do the job wrong. See the [Wisconsin bat exclusion law guide](/guides/wisconsin-bat-exclusion-law/) and [why you cannot evict bats in June and July](/blog/wisconsin-bat-maternity-season-explained/).

Native [woodpeckers](/pest-library/woodpeckers/) and most native birds are separately protected under federal law, which means the toolkit there is deterrence, exclusion, insect control and repair — never removal.

## Commercial bird work

A large share of what we do in Appleton. [Starlings, pigeons and house sparrows](/pest-library/nuisance-birds/) on signage, ledges, awnings, vents and rooftop units.

This is an exclusion project, not a spray: sealing entries, netting or screening roost areas, deterrents where they land. Those three species are not federally protected, though almost every other bird is.

The part people do not anticipate: the nests left behind carry **bird mites**, which move indoors looking for a host when the birds leave. That is how a bird problem on a roof becomes a complaint about biting insects in the office below. See [commercial pest control in Appleton](/service-area/appleton/commercial/).

## What a job runs through

1. **Inspect from outside first.** The entry is found by reading the roofline and the sign around it, not by starting in the attic. There is usually more than one, and the ones nobody found are why a first attempt fails.
2. **Identify species and season**, which decides what is legal and what is humane.
3. **Get them out**, usually with one-way devices, so the animal leaves to forage and cannot return.
4. **Confirm the space is empty** before anything is closed.
5. **Exclude properly** — hardware cloth and flashing, mechanically fastened, not foam in a hole. The whole roofline, not the one opening.
6. **Clean up what needs it**, and say honestly what does not.
