## Close enough to look before you commit

Embarrass is six miles north of the shop, and on wildlife work that proximity is worth more than it is on a pest job.

Here is why. **The most valuable part of a wildlife job is the inspection**, and specifically finding *every* entry point rather than the obvious one. Almost every failed first attempt looks the same: somebody removed the animal, sealed the hole they could see, and left the two they did not find. Being ten minutes away means we can come and read the roofline properly before anybody commits to a plan or a price.

## What turns up around here

The village sits on the **Embarrass River**, ringed by farmsteads and rural property, which gives a fairly predictable cast.

**[Bats](/pest-library/bats/)** — older farmhouses along the river have the deep attics and open soffit runs bats want, and a colony grows quietly for seasons before the numbers become obvious. Squeaking and a dry rustle at dusk from a wall or the peak, staining at an exterior gap, droppings collecting below it.

**[Raccoons](/pest-library/raccoons/)** — attics, chimneys, outbuildings, and under decks and sheds. Heavy, deliberate movement after dark. Strong, destructive, and between March and June likely to have a litter somewhere behind them.

**[Squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/)** — scratching and rolling at dawn and dusk, silence overnight. Roof edges and soffits, usually where an ice dam already opened the fascia. The wiring is the part to take seriously.

**[Skunks](/pest-library/skunks/) and [groundhogs](/pest-library/groundhogs/)** — under decks, stoops, sheds and granary floors. Look at the entry before sealing anything.

**[Muskrats](/pest-library/muskrats/) and [beavers](/pest-library/beavers/)** — river corridor animals, and a different kind of problem: bank damage, blocked culverts, and flooded low ground rather than anything in a building.

The time of day the noise happens does most of the identification before anybody climbs. [The longer version](/blog/squirrels-raccoons-in-the-attic/).

## Bats have a legally binding calendar

Wisconsin protects its bats, and exclusion is prohibited during the summer maternity season while flightless pups are in the roost. Seal then and the pups die inside the structure — illegal, inhumane, and a considerably worse problem for you than the colony was, because what follows is odor and insects inside the insulation for months.

So a June call gets an inspection, a full plan and a booked date rather than immediate work. Detail: the [Wisconsin bat exclusion law guide](/guides/wisconsin-bat-exclusion-law/) and [why you cannot evict bats in June and July](/blog/wisconsin-bat-maternity-season-explained/).

[Woodpeckers](/pest-library/woodpeckers/) and most native birds are separately protected under federal law, so that work is deterrence, exclusion, insect control and repair — never removal. Frequently the bird is there because there are insects in the siding, which is the actual thing to fix.

## Removal is the easy half

Trap tonight's raccoon and the torn soffit it climbed through is still open next week — and it is the same opening that gives you [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/) in October, [wasps](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) in July and [cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) in September.

One repair closes all of it, which is why we quote the whole job rather than the removal alone.

**The sequence:** inspect from outside first; identify species and season, because that decides what is legal and humane; get them out with one-way devices where possible; **confirm the space is empty before closing anything**; exclude with hardware cloth and flashing that is mechanically fastened, along the whole roofline rather than at the one hole; then clean up what needs it and say honestly what does not.
