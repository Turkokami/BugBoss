## Three different towns in one zip code

For pest work, Clintonville is not one place. It is three, and which one your house sits in determines almost everything about what you will deal with and what will fix it.

**The century-old blocks near downtown and along Main.** Balloon-framed walls, original soffit runs, settled trim, deep attics, and a hundred years of small openings that nobody has ever fully closed. These houses get the heaviest fall-invader loads in the village and the most puzzling symptoms — insects appearing at a second-floor window in a house where nothing ever gets in on the ground floor.

**The mid-century neighborhoods.** Tighter shells, simpler rooflines, and generally a much easier building to seal. Their weak points are predictable rather than pervasive: the attached garage that was never sealed at the slab joint, soffit vents whose screening has failed, the utility penetrations behind the meter, and window seals that have given up after fifty winters.

**The farm properties on the edge of town.** Here the house is not really the problem. The field, the outbuildings, and the feed are, and any work that stops at the back door will get undone by the pole barn within a season.

We are based at **600 S. Main St.**, which means we know which of the three you are in before we get there.

## What a Clintonville pest year actually looks like

The work here runs on a calendar you could set a watch by.

**Late August into September** is the single most important window of the year, and almost nobody acts in it. This is when [boxelder bugs](/pest-library/boxelder-bugs/), [cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/), [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) and [stink bugs](/pest-library/brown-marmorated-stink-bugs/) begin massing on warm south and west walls looking for a way in. An exterior treatment applied *before* they arrive works well. The identical treatment applied in late October, after they are in the wall, does very little — and that timing gap is the single most common reason people conclude that treatment does not work.

**October and November** is rodents. [Mice](/pest-library/mice/) move in off the fields and the ditches as the nights drop, and they need about a quarter inch. The frost that opened your foundation joints last winter is still open.

**Winter** is mostly quiet, except for the fall invaders waking up on warm days and appearing at the sunny windows. There is nothing to treat at that point — they have been in the wall since September. [Here is what is actually happening](/blog/bugs-indoors-in-january/).

**Spring** is [carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ants/), particularly along the Pigeon River and anywhere with mature trees, and the first ant foraging. Large black ants active *indoors* in February or March usually mean a nest inside the structure, because the outdoor colonies are still dormant — which makes it worth investigating rather than waiting out.

**July through September** is [wasps and hornets](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) on eaves, porch ceilings, outbuildings and in the ground. A nest that was the size of a walnut in May is the size of a dinner plate by August, which is why the calls all arrive at once.

## What we actually do at a house here

**Inspect inside and out**, and mean the outside part. Most durable pest control in this village is carpentry: the foundation line, the sill plate, the utility penetrations, the window wells, the soffit and fascia, the roof edge where the ice dams did their work last winter.

**Treat the specific problem**, not the house. A blanket spray on a schedule is easy to sell and it is not what most of these homes need.

**Seal the entry points**, which is the part with lasting value because it works on everything using the same opening. The gap that lets in cluster flies in September lets in mice in November and gave the wasps a soffit void in July. One repair.

**Say when you do not need us.** Springtails and millipedes in a damp basement are a moisture problem, and the honest answer is a dehumidifier and a downspout extension rather than a program. You will hear that from us.
