## Two towns in one, divided by the water

Menasha sits at the north end of **Lake Winnebago**, on the Fox River channel and the locks, and for pest work it functions as two different places.

**Near the water** is the older stock — rental housing, mill-era buildings that have been converted and subdivided, duplexes and small apartment buildings, and the tight blocks on and around Doty Island. Here the defining feature is **shared structure**, and it changes the work more than anything else about the property.

**Away from the water** are the postwar single-family neighborhoods, which get the ordinary Fox Valley calendar — ants in spring, wasps through summer, mice in fall, fall invaders on the sunny walls in late September — and respond well to the standard exterior-plus-exclusion approach.

## If you are in a shared building, scope is the whole game

In a shared structure, the boundary between "your problem" and "the building's problem" is not the wall. It is nothing at all.

The pattern is consistent enough to predict. Somebody reports [bed bugs](/pest-library/bed-bugs/). Their unit gets treated. Six weeks later the unit next door reports them. That gets treated. By the second year the landlord thinks the tenants keep bringing them in and the tenants think the landlord is using a company that cannot do the job.

Neither is being unreasonable, and neither is right. The population is **moving through the building** — along plumbing chases, electrical runs, shared wall voids and hallways — always one step ahead of a treatment aimed at one unit at a time. Every treatment worked. The scope was wrong.

Same mechanism for [cockroaches](/pest-library/cockroaches/), and for [mice](/pest-library/mice/) using the same routes plus the gaps around old plumbing penetrations between floors.

**What to do if you rent**, regardless of how the responsibility question resolves:

- **Report it in writing immediately** and keep a copy. Verbal reports vanish.
- **Do not treat it quietly yourself.** A retail spray scatters the population into neighboring units and makes the eventual professional job harder.
- **Do not move furniture or a mattress** into another unit, a hallway or a storage room. This is the most reliable way to spread it.
- **Do not throw out the mattress** before getting advice — an encasement traps what is inside and it dies there, and dragging an infested mattress down a shared stairwell seeds the building.

Our [Wisconsin landlord and tenant](/compliance/landlord-tenant-pest-control/) and [bed bugs in rentals](/compliance/bed-bugs-in-rentals/) pages set out the responsibility question properly.

## The mill-era buildings

The older stock near the channel has the structural features that make this work harder: **open and shared wall cavities**, **old plumbing chases** running vertically with unsealed floor penetrations, **additions meeting original structure** at joints that have settled and opened, and **settled masonry and original window openings** at the foundation.

Those connections are exactly what lets pests move between units without ever crossing a doorway. It also means exclusion in these buildings is genuine construction work — hardware cloth, flashing, mortar, mechanical fasteners — rather than a tube of caulk on a Saturday. It is the part with lasting value, because it works on every pest using the same opening.

## What the lake can and cannot be fixed

**The midge and mayfly hatches are not a pest control problem.** They come off open water in enormous numbers on their own schedule, they do not breed on your property, and nothing sprayed on a building affects a hatch. Anyone selling you a treatment for them is selling something that will not work. What helps is screening, exterior lighting changes so the building is less of a beacon, and cleanup.

**Mosquitoes are the opposite** — they breed in standing water on your own property, and that responds well. The clogged gutter, the tarp, the tire, the boat cover, the wheelbarrow, the saucer under a planter, the low spot that holds water a week after rain. Source reduction plus treating the shaded resting sites where adults spend the day. [Mosquito and tick control](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/).

**The damp-basement pests** — [springtails](/pest-library/springtails/), [sowbugs](/pest-library/sowbugs-pillbugs/), [millipedes](/pest-library/millipedes/), [centipedes](/pest-library/centipedes/) — come with the water table. They are moisture indicators first, and the honest fix is a dehumidifier, downspouts, grading and a vapor barrier rather than a program.

## The single-family calendar

Away from the water, Menasha runs the standard Fox Valley year:

- **April–May** — [carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ants/) where wood has been wet, and wasp queens starting nests still the size of a walnut.
- **July–September** — [wasps and hornets](/residential-pest-control/stinging-insects/) at colony peak. [Which nests can wait](/blog/wasp-hornet-nest-when-to-call/).
- **Late August–September** — the [fall invader](/residential-pest-control/fall-invaders/) window, and the single most important two weeks of the year. Before they mass on the siding, not after.
- **October–December** — [rodents](/residential-pest-control/rodent-control/). A quarter inch is enough.
- **January–February** — nothing to treat. What is at the window has been in the wall since September and cannot breed indoors. [Why](/blog/bugs-indoors-in-january/).
