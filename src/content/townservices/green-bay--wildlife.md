## This is the actual reason we come to Green Bay

Everywhere else on this site we are fairly blunt that a Brown County company will beat us on response time for a routine call. This page is the exception, and it is worth explaining why.

**Most pest control companies serving the Green Bay metro do not do wildlife at all.** They will treat insects and rodents, and when there is an animal in a wall they refer it out — to a trapper who removes the animal and leaves, or to nobody. Call around about a raccoon and you will find the pattern within three phone calls.

That is not incompetence, it is business model. Wildlife needs roof work, building repair, species-by-species legal knowledge, and a willingness to schedule around an animal rather than a route. Recurring chemical service does not have room for it.

Out in the rural corridor there has never been anyone to refer it to, so we learned to do both halves. And because a wildlife job is **scheduled work rather than an emergency**, the 45-minute drive costs you nothing in response time.

## Removal is the easy half

Almost every call arrives as "get this animal out," and almost every one is really "close this hole."

Take tonight's [raccoon](/pest-library/raccoons/) and the torn soffit it climbed through is still open next week — and it is the same opening that gives you [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/) in October, [wasps](/residential-pest-control/stinging-insects/) in July, and every [fall invader](/service-area/green-bay/residential/) on the property in September. One repair closes all of it, which is why we quote the whole job rather than the removal alone.

**The sequence:** inspect from the outside first, because the entry is found by reading the roofline and the sign around it and there is nearly always more than one. Identify species and season, which decides what is legal and humane. Get them out with one-way devices where possible. **Confirm the space is empty before closing anything.** Exclude with heavy-gauge hardware cloth and metal flashing, mechanically fastened — not foam in a hole, which a determined animal removes overnight. Then clean up what needs it, and say honestly what does not.

## What turns up in the city

**[Raccoons](/pest-library/raccoons/)** — attics, chimneys, under decks and porches. Heavy deliberate movement after dark. The most destructive, the most likely to injure a person or a dog, and the most likely to have a litter behind them in spring.

**[Squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/)** — scratching and rolling at dawn and dusk, silence overnight. Roof edges and soffits, usually where an ice dam already lifted the fascia. Chewed wiring is the part to take seriously: rodent-damaged insulation on a live conductor is a fire risk and not a wait-and-see item.

**[Bats](/pest-library/bats/)** — older near-downtown and near-east housing has the deep attics and open soffit runs they want. Squeaking and a dry rustle at dusk, greasy staining at an exterior gap, droppings collecting below it. Legal calendar applies.

**[Skunks](/pest-library/skunks/) and [groundhogs](/pest-library/groundhogs/)** — under decks, stoops, sheds and porches. Look at the entry, and for young, before closing anything.

**[Nuisance birds](/pest-library/nuisance-birds/)** — starlings, pigeons and house sparrows on signage, parapets, ledges and loading canopies. Non-native and not federally protected, which is exactly why identification comes first: native birds *are* protected and acting on a misidentification is a federal violation.

Identification by sound, before anybody climbs: [reading the noise](/blog/squirrels-raccoons-in-the-attic/).

## Bats: the calendar is binding

Wisconsin protects its bats, and exclusion is prohibited during the summer maternity season while flightless pups are in the roost. Sealing then kills the pups inside the structure — illegal, inhumane, and a considerably worse problem for you than the colony was.

So a June or July call gets an inspection, a full plan and a booked date rather than immediate work. We would rather explain that and lose the momentum than do it wrong. Detail: the [Wisconsin bat exclusion law guide](/guides/wisconsin-bat-exclusion-law/) and [why you cannot evict bats in June and July](/blog/wisconsin-bat-maternity-season-explained/).

[Woodpeckers](/pest-library/woodpeckers/) and most native birds are separately protected under federal law, so that work is deterrence, exclusion, insect control and repair — never removal. Frequently the bird is there because there are insects in the siding, which is the real fix.
