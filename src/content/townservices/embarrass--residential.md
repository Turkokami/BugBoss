## Ten minutes from the shop, and on a river

Two facts shape pest work in Embarrass, and both are geographic.

The first is **proximity**. The village is six miles north of 600 S. Main St. in Clintonville, which makes it one of the only places outside the village itself where same-day response for an ordinary call is realistic rather than aspirational. If something needs looking at before you decide whether to spend money on it, that is not a problem here.

The second is **the Embarrass River**. Riverside and low-lying property holds moisture longer than ground elsewhere in the county, and moisture is the underlying cause of a whole category of calls that people mistake for insect problems.

## The moisture pests, and why they are not really pest problems

If your Embarrass basement produces [springtails](/pest-library/springtails/), [millipedes](/pest-library/millipedes/), [sowbugs](/pest-library/sowbugs-pillbugs/) or [centipedes](/pest-library/centipedes/) every spring, the useful thing to know is that **none of them can establish indoors unless it is damp.** They eat mold, algae and decaying plant matter. A thriving indoor population means a surface has been wet long enough to grow their food.

So the honest answer is usually not a treatment. It is a dehumidifier holding humidity below about fifty percent, downspouts extended well clear of the wall, a regrade so the ground falls away from the house, mulch pulled back to leave a dry inspection strip, and a vapor barrier over bare crawlspace soil. Seal the ground-level gaps too — but the drying is what makes it stick.

We would rather tell you that than put you on a quarterly program for an insect that is only there because water is going somewhere it should not.

## Carpenter ants and the same underlying problem

[Carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ants/) are common along the river for exactly the same reason. They do not eat wood — they excavate it, and they excavate wood that has been **wet**. A carpenter ant problem is therefore usually a moisture problem wearing a disguise.

Which means the job has two parts: find and treat the nest, then find and tell you about the water. A gutter, a grade, a flashing detail, a sill that a deck ledger has been trapping water against, a leak under a bathroom. Treating without fixing the water means doing it again in eighteen months.

Worth knowing: large black ants active **indoors** in February or March generally mean a nest inside the structure, because outdoor colonies are still dormant. That is worth investigating rather than waiting out.

## Mosquitoes: your yard matters more than the river

People assume the river is the mosquito source and that there is nothing to be done. Both halves of that are mostly wrong.

The mosquitoes biting you in your own yard are largely breeding **in your own yard** — in container water. The clogged gutter, the tarp, the tire, the boat cover, the wheelbarrow, the saucer under a planter, the low spot that holds water for a week after rain. A surprising amount of relief comes from an hour of tipping things over.

Beyond that, treating the shaded resting sites where adults spend the day — dense shrubs, the yard edge, under decks — does more than anything aimed at open water. [Mosquito and tick control](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/).

## The rest of the calendar

**Late August into early September** — the fall invader window, and the most important two weeks of the year. [Cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) treated on the exterior *before* they mass on the siding, plus sealing. After they are in the wall it is largely too late for that year. [What is actually happening in there](/blog/bugs-indoors-in-january/).

**October and November** — [mice](/pest-library/mice/) off the fields. A quarter inch is enough, and the frost that moved your foundation last winter left the doors open.

**July through September** — [wasps and hornets](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) on eaves, outbuildings and in the ground. [Which nests can wait and which cannot](/blog/wasp-hornet-nest-when-to-call/).

**Spring** — the thaw walk-around, which on a rural property is the most revealing hour of the year. [What to look for](/blog/spring-thaw-pest-check/).

## Farm buildings count

Most Embarrass properties have an outbuilding, and any work that stops at the back door gets undone by it. A pole barn or machine shed with feed in it is a rodent reservoir a hundred feet away, and the metal skirting is the usual failure — it lifts, equipment bends it, and the gap runs the length of the wall. [The pole barn problem](/blog/mice-in-the-pole-barn/) and the [farmhouse rodent exclusion guide](/guides/farmhouse-rodent-exclusion/).
