## Water decides most of it

Manawa sits on the **Little Wolf River**, with **Bear Lake** just north and low ground either side. Almost everything distinctive about pest work here follows from that.

There are really three property types in the village and around it — **river and lake homes, village housing, and the surrounding farms** — and while they share a calendar, water changes the emphasis for the first group substantially.

## Mosquitoes: the part people give up on too early

The usual assumption is that the river and the lake produce the mosquitoes and there is nothing to be done. Both halves are mostly wrong.

The mosquitoes biting you in your own yard are largely breeding **in your own yard**, in container water — the clogged gutter, the tarp over the boat, the tire, the wheelbarrow, the saucer under a planter, the tipped-over kayak, the low spot that holds water for a week after rain. Several species complete a generation in under two weeks in a few tablespoons of water. An hour spent tipping things over in June does more than most people expect.

Beyond that, the productive treatment target is the **shaded resting sites** where adult mosquitoes spend the day — dense shrubs, the yard edge, under decks — rather than open water, which is neither practical nor lawful to treat wholesale. [Mosquito and tick control](/residential-pest-control/mosquito-tick/).

## Cluster flies and the river-facing wall

River-facing homes here get hit hard by [cluster flies](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [boxelder bugs](/pest-library/boxelder-bugs/), and two things stack to cause it.

Cluster fly larvae develop inside **earthworms in the soil**, and the damp, worm-rich ground of a river corridor supports a large local population. Then, in September, a house facing open water presents an unobstructed sunny wall that warms in the afternoon — precisely the surface the flies orient to. Nothing about it relates to how the house is kept.

The intervention is timed rather than reactive: an **exterior treatment in late August or early September**, before they arrive on the siding, plus sealing the seams. In late October, when they are in the wall voids, that same treatment does very little. [What is happening in the wall all winter](/blog/bugs-indoors-in-january/).

## The damp-basement pests

A high water table produces [springtails](/pest-library/springtails/), [sowbugs](/pest-library/sowbugs-pillbugs/), [millipedes](/pest-library/millipedes/) and [centipedes](/pest-library/centipedes/), and the useful thing to know is that **none of them can persist indoors unless it is damp.** They eat mold, algae and decaying matter. A recurring population means a surface has been wet long enough to grow their food.

So the honest recommendation is generally a dehumidifier below about fifty percent, downspouts extended clear of the wall, a regrade, mulch pulled back to leave a dry inspection strip, and a vapor barrier over bare crawlspace soil — plus sealing the ground-level gaps. The drying is what makes it hold.

## The rest of the year

**October and November** — [mice](/pest-library/mice/) off the fields and the ditches. A quarter inch is enough.

**July through September** — [wasps and hornets](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/). Around open grassy ground, the ones that cause real trouble are **ground-nesting yellowjackets**, invisible until a mower or a weed-whip sets them off. Walking the edges before you mow in August is worth two minutes. [Which nests can wait](/blog/wasp-hornet-nest-when-to-call/).

**Spring** — [carpenter ants](/pest-library/carpenter-ants/) wherever wood has stayed wet, which near water is a recurring theme, and the thaw walk-around that shows you the whole winter's rodent traffic before the grass grows back. [The March walk](/blog/spring-thaw-pest-check/).

## Seasonal and lake property

Buildings that sit empty need a different rhythm: **two well-timed visits** — closing in the fall and opening in spring — rather than a quarterly schedule on a building nobody occupies. The closing visit is the one that matters, because whatever gets in during October has until May undisturbed and unnoticed. [Cabin and seasonal pest control](/guides/cabin-seasonal-pest-control/) and the [closing checklist](/blog/cabin-closing-pest-checklist/).

## Farms around the village

On a farm property the house is downstream of the operation: the fields, the outbuildings and the feed generate the pressure, and work that stops at the back door gets undone by the pole barn.
