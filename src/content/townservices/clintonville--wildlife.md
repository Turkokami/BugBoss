## Why there is so much of this work in Clintonville

The village has exactly the housing stock wildlife wants. Century-old homes with deep attics, generous original soffit runs, and rooflines that a hundred Wisconsin winters have quietly opened up. Add mature trees along the Pigeon River giving squirrels a route onto the roof, and farm outbuildings on the edge of town giving everything a place to overwinter, and the result is steady wildlife work in a village of a few thousand people.

The other reason is supply. Most pest control companies — including the metro franchises that occasionally route a truck through here — do not do wildlife at all. They will treat insects and rodents and refer an animal in a wall out to somebody else, or to nobody. Out here there frequently is nobody else, which is why BugBoss does both halves.

## Removal is the easy part. The building is the job.

Almost every wildlife call arrives as a request to take an animal out of a building, and almost every one of them is really a request to close a hole.

Trap tonight's raccoon and the torn soffit it climbed through is still open next week. So is the gap at the fascia the [squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/) will find in October, the same joint the [wasps](/pest-library/wasps-hornets/) will build in next July, and the opening every [cluster fly](/pest-library/cluster-flies/) and [lady beetle](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) on the property will use in September.

One repair closes all of it. That is why what we sell is not really removal — it is **getting the animals out, closing the building, and cleaning up what got left behind.**

## The animals, and how to tell them apart before anyone climbs

The **time of day** the noise happens does most of the identification.

- **Dawn and dusk, scratching and a rolling sound, silence overnight** — [gray squirrels](/pest-library/squirrels/). They are strictly daytime animals, and the rolling is an acorn being moved across the joists.
- **After dark, heavy and deliberate, with dragging** — [raccoons](/pest-library/raccoons/). If it sounds like a person is up there, it probably is one of these. The most destructive of the group and the one most likely to injure someone; do not go up.
- **Fast, light, constant, at night, in walls as well as overhead** — [mice](/pest-library/mice/).
- **Squeaking and a dry rustle at dusk, from a wall or soffit, with staining at an exterior gap** — [bats](/pest-library/bats/).
- **A smell and no noise at all** — something died in a void. Blow flies in the living space are usually how people find out.

We wrote the longer version of this up here: [reading the noise before you call anyone](/blog/squirrels-raccoons-in-the-attic/).

## Bats: the calendar is not optional

Wisconsin protects its bats, and exclusion is prohibited during the summer maternity season while flightless pups are in the roost. Sealing a roost then kills the pups inside the structure — illegal, inhumane, and a considerably worse problem for the homeowner than the colony was.

So a bat call in June gets an inspection, a full plan, and a booked date rather than immediate work. We would rather tell you that and lose the momentum than do the job wrong. The detail is in our [Wisconsin bat exclusion law guide](/guides/wisconsin-bat-exclusion-law/) and the post on [why you cannot evict bats in June and July](/blog/wisconsin-bat-maternity-season-explained/).

Native woodpeckers and most native birds are separately protected under federal law, which means the toolkit for a [woodpecker](/pest-library/woodpeckers/) on your siding is deterrence, exclusion, insect control and repair — never removal.

## What a job runs through

1. **Inspect from outside first.** The entry is found by reading the roofline and the sign around it, not by starting in the attic. There is usually more than one, and the ones nobody found are why a first attempt fails.
2. **Identify species and season** — which decides what is legal and what is humane.
3. **Get them out**, usually with one-way devices, so the animal leaves to forage and cannot return.
4. **Confirm the space is empty** before anything is closed.
5. **Exclude properly** — hardware cloth and flashing, mechanically fastened, not foam in a hole. The whole roofline, not the one opening.
6. **Clean up what needs it**, and say honestly what does not.
