Appleton's mosquito problem is a terrain problem, and the river most people would point at is largely innocent.

Before anything else: **we are not an Appleton company.** The shop is in Clintonville. For planned seasonal work that is fine; for a same-day emergency a Fox Valley company will be there first and we will tell you to call one.

## The Fox is not breeding them

The Fox through Appleton drops through a gorge with locks. It moves. **Larvae cannot complete development in moving water**, so the river is not the nursery.

What the river does supply is humidity and shade, both of which let adult mosquitoes survive longer. That is an effect and a secondary one.

## The ravines are the actual story

Appleton is cut by ravines and creek corridors, and they run right up to the back of residential lots across the city. A ravine supplies both halves of the mosquito equation at once:

- **The bottom holds water** in pockets after rain — shallow, shaded, undisturbed, fish-free
- **The sides are dense, humid and shaded**, which is precisely where adults spend daylight before coming out at dusk

A lot backing onto one of those corridors is adjacent to a permanent reservoir of harborage. It will always run worse than a lot in the middle of a flat open block, and that is topography rather than anything the owner has got wrong.

The creek corridors on the north and west edges work the same way at a larger scale: lower ground, wetter margins, more unmown vegetation, and subdivisions backing straight onto them.

## Your own yard, which is still most of it

The on-lot sources are the same anywhere, and on a city lot they are frequently the entire problem:

- Gutters, clogged — the most common source we find, and the one nobody checks
- Corrugated downspout extensions holding water in the ribs
- A tarp over the boat, trailer or woodpile with a pocket in it
- Plant saucers, buckets, wheelbarrows, bin lids
- A birdbath nobody empties, and tires behind the garage
- The low patch of lawn that stays soft a week after a storm

Some species need a bottle cap. A week of undisturbed water is the entire life cycle. Clearing this list costs nothing and we would rather you did it before paying anyone.

## Why a yard fog is the wrong product

Adults do not spend the day in open mown grass. They rest in **shaded vegetation**: shrub undersides, dense ground cover, tall grass at the lot line, the north side of the house and garage, the ravine or creek edge.

That band is what a barrier treatment is for, and on a ravine lot it is a well-defined band — which is exactly why treatment makes a visible difference on those properties and much less on a bare open lot.

A company that quotes a lawn fog without walking the property has not looked at where the mosquitoes are.

## Timing beats frequency

Rain triggers everything. A significant rainfall refills every container and pocket simultaneously, and roughly a week later a **synchronised hatch** arrives. A treatment landing in that window is worth two on a schedule.

Most Appleton properties do well on a small number of well-timed applications plus the source reduction, rather than a standing monthly visit.
