Clintonville is home base — the BugBoss storefront is at 600 S. Main St. — so we see more cluster fly work in this town than anywhere else on the map, and we see it in two distinct versions.

## The village houses

The older homes around downtown and the mid-century neighbourhoods behind them are the harder buildings. Cluster flies do not need much: a gap at a soffit return, a worn seal on an original wood window, a crack where the frieze board meets the wall. A century-old Clintonville house has dozens of those, and balloon framing means once the flies are into a wall cavity they can travel almost the full height of the building.

That is why people in the older parts of town describe finding them in an upstairs bedroom in February with no idea how they got there. They got in in September, three storeys of wall cavity away.

## The properties on the edge

Head out past the last streets in any direction and you are in open ground almost immediately. That matters because **cluster fly larvae develop in earthworms**, so the population is a product of soil, lawn and pasture rather than of anything in your house. A property surrounded by mown grass and field is producing flies whether or not the building is sealed.

Homes along the **Pigeon River** corridor get it especially hard — river-bottom soil holds moisture, moisture supports earthworms, and earthworms support cluster flies.

The practical consequence: on these properties, sealing alone will never be enough. There is simply too much pressure arriving at the wall each autumn. What works is cutting the numbers on the exterior before they get to the gaps.

## The timing, which is the whole job

Cluster fly work in Clintonville is a **calendar problem more than a technical one**. The window is late August into the first half of September, before they start massing. Miss it and there is very little worth doing until next year.

Being in town helps here in a way that is genuinely practical rather than a marketing line — we can slot Clintonville properties into the early part of the season without a drive, which means more of our local customers actually get treated inside the window instead of two weeks after it.

## What we do

We treat the south and west elevations and the soffit line where they gather, timed to the season rather than to the phone call. We seal what is worth sealing — soffit returns, window frames, utility penetrations, the roofline junctions — and we are honest about what is not worth chasing on a hundred-year-old house.

And we handle the rest of the fall-invader complex on the same visit, because [boxelder bugs](/pest-library/boxelder-bugs/) and [Asian lady beetles](/pest-library/asian-lady-beetles/) are on the same walls on the same afternoons for the same reason. Treating one and not the others makes no sense when the exterior work is identical.
