Kaukauna is a river town with an unusual amount of slow, shaded water edge for its size, and that is the whole explanation for its mosquito pressure.

## A chain of pools, not a river

The Fox drops through Kaukauna across a run of **locks**, and that changes the water's behavior.

A fast channel is poor mosquito habitat — larvae cannot develop in current. But locks and drops break a fast river into a series of **slower pools**, and a slow pool with a weedy margin sheltered from wind is a genuinely good nursery.

The structures are not the problem. The quiet margin they create is, and Kaukauna has more of it per mile of river than a town where the Fox simply runs past.

## The island corridor

The other half is the **wooded river island** ground inside the city.

A wooded corridor supplies exactly what adult mosquitoes need during the day: dense shade, humidity, and undisturbed vegetation. It also holds water in low pockets after the river level moves.

A property facing or backing onto that corridor sits beside a standing reservoir of harborage and runs noticeably worse than a lot a few blocks up the hill. It is also one of the nicer places in the Fox Valley to live, which is the trade being made.

## What the river is and is not doing

Not breeding them. The flowing channel produces nothing.

What it does: raises humidity and supplies shade, so adults that hatched elsewhere survive longer. And it creates the still margins, backwater pockets and low ground that genuinely do produce.

## Your own lot, which is most of the controllable share

- Gutters, clogged — the most common source anywhere, almost never inspected
- Corrugated downspout extensions holding water in the ribs
- A tarp over the boat, trailer or woodpile with a pocket in it
- Plant saucers, buckets, wheelbarrows, bin lids, tires
- A birdbath nobody empties
- The low patch of lawn that stays soft for a week after a storm

A week of undisturbed water is the whole life cycle. Some species need a bottle cap. Clearing this list costs nothing and on a city lot it is frequently most of the answer.

## Why a lawn fog is the wrong product

Adults are not in the open grass during the day. They rest in **shaded vegetation** — shrub undersides, dense ground cover, tall grass at the lot line, the shaded side of the house and garage, the river or island margin.

On a Kaukauna riverside lot that band is well defined, which is precisely why a proper barrier treatment makes a visible difference there and a lawn fog does not.

## Timing

Rain is the trigger. A significant rainfall refills everything at once, and a **synchronised hatch** follows about a week later. A treatment landing in that window is worth two applied on a calendar.
