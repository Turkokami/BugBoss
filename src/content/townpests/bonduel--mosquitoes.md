Bonduel is a village of a few hundred people with dairy ground running out in every direction, and the mosquito problem here belongs to the farmland rather than the village.

## The farmyard is the hatchery

The productive water on a working property in this part of Shawano County is almost never the water you would point at.

**Bunker silo covers** are the standout. The tires weighting the plastic each hold a reservoir of rainwater in the rim, there are frequently dozens of them, and nobody tips them from one year to the next. By midsummer a single bunker can be producing more mosquitoes than every container in the village combined.

After that, in rough order of how often we find them:

- Stock tanks, and the churned, pooled ground around them
- Implement cavities and upturned equipment left out
- Buckets, feed pans, mineral tubs
- The ditch along the lane, and the tile outlet at the end of it
- Roof runoff off an outbuilding with no gutter
- Any low spot in a lot or yard that holds for a week after rain

A week of undisturbed water is the entire life cycle. Some species need no more than a bottle cap.

## What the lagoon is and is not

A common misdiagnosis out here. The **manure lagoon itself is generally not producing** — the chemistry is hostile to larvae.

What produces is the clean water around it: runoff pockets, ponded ground at the edge of the lot, rainwater standing in equipment ruts. People treat the lagoon as the obvious suspect and walk past the actual source twenty feet away.

## The fence row is where the adults are

Adults do not spend daylight in the open. They sit in **cool shaded vegetation**, and on a Bonduel farmstead that means the fence rows, the treelines, the unmown margins, the shaded north side of the machine shed and the pole barn.

That is where a barrier treatment belongs. On a farm property the treated band runs around the edge of the operation, not around the house — which is why the area we treat looks nothing like the yard where you were being bitten.

## Livestock and feed set the rules

This governs the job rather than complicating it. Product goes where the label allows and nowhere near feed, feed storage, water or animal access. On a working operation that constrains both where and when, and it gets worked out with you before anything is applied.

If a property cannot be treated safely in a way that would actually help, that is worth hearing before you pay for a visit rather than after.

## When you do not need us

On a quarter-acre village lot in Bonduel, the walk-round usually is the answer: gutters, the tarp on the woodpile, the wheelbarrow, the plant saucers, the downspout extension that holds water in the ribs. If clearing those settles it, you have saved the cost of a visit and we would rather you did.

A treatment earns its place where there is real shaded harborage, or where the lot backs onto a field and is being reloaded from ground you do not own.
