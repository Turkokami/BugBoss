Almost everybody assumes Green Bay's mosquitoes come off the bay. They mostly do not, and getting that wrong leads to spending money in the wrong place.

## The bay is not the nursery

Mosquito larvae need water that is **shallow, warm, still and fish-free.** The bay is large, windy and full of fish. Wave action alone makes most of it useless as habitat.

What produces along the shore is the **sheltered wetland margin** — and that is a shoreline phenomenon, not a city-wide one.

## The corridors are

Inside the city the real mosquito geography is the creek and greenway network: the **East River**, **Baird Creek**, **Duck Creek**, and the wooded strips running between neighborhoods.

A corridor supplies both halves of the problem:

- **Intermittent standing water** — low pockets, backwater, ground that holds after a rise
- **Continuous shaded vegetation**, which is where adults spend daylight before coming out at dusk

A lot backing onto one of those sits beside the full package. Distance from a corridor predicts a Green Bay property's mosquito pressure far better than which side of town it is on.

This is good news, incidentally: a corridor is a defined edge that a treatment can address. A bay is not.

## Why your back yard is a different place from your front

Because the harborage is at the back.

Adults are not in open mown grass during the day. They are in cool shaded vegetation, so a lot with a sunny open front and a brushy shaded rear boundary is genuinely two environments. You walk into the harborage and the harborage responds.

That is also why a barrier treatment goes on the rear band rather than across the lawn, and why the treated area looks nothing like the patio where you were being bitten.

## Older neighborhoods versus newer ones

Two effects pulling opposite ways.

The **older east-side neighborhoods** have mature trees and dense established planting, so more shaded harborage per lot and more adults holding through the day. But their grading and drainage have had a century to settle, so they often produce less.

**Newer subdivisions** have the reverse: little harborage, but unsettled grade, compacted soil, low spots that were not meant to be low spots, and basins that may or may not drain.

Which effect dominates is a property-by-property question, which is why the walk matters more than the neighborhood.

## The on-lot list

- Gutters, clogged — the most common source anywhere, by a wide margin
- Corrugated downspout extensions holding water in the ribs
- A tarp over the boat, trailer or woodpile
- Plant saucers, buckets, wheelbarrows, bin lids, tires
- A birdbath nobody empties
- The low patch of lawn that stays soft a week after a storm

Some species need a bottle cap's worth. A week of undisturbed water completes the cycle.

## What we will not do

Treat the greenway behind your house. It is public ground, it is not ours, and anyone offering is overreaching.

What we will do is treat your side of the boundary — which is where the adults are coming to spend the day — and clear what is producing inside your lot line. On a corridor-adjacent property that combination makes a clear difference in the evening without pretending the corridor is gone.
