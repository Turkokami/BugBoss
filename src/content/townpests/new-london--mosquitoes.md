New London sits at the confluence of the **Embarrass** and the **Wolf**, and the floodplain around that junction is why this is one of the heaviest mosquito towns in the corridor. The local nature center is called **Mosquito Hill**. The problem has been here longer than any of us.

## Floodplain is the best mosquito habitat there is

Not because of the rivers. Flowing water produces nothing — larvae cannot complete development in it.

The engine is what a flood leaves behind. When high water recedes it strands **hundreds of isolated pools** across the floodplain: shallow, warm, fish-free, undisturbed, full of organic matter. That is close to a perfect nursery, and there is no realistic way to address it.

Floodwater mosquito species are specifically adapted to the cycle. Eggs are laid in soil that is dry at the time and hatch when the next flood wets them, which is why a wet spring followed by a wet June produces far worse than the rainfall totals suggest.

## The spring pulse decides the year

The season here does not start on a date. It starts when the water drops.

Floodwater recedes, the stranded pools warm, and a large **synchronised cohort** emerges a week or two later. Every significant rain after that adds another, on the same lag. Treatment timed to the pulse is worth several applications made on a fixed interval, and that is how we schedule in New London.

## Backwaters and sloughs

Off the main channels there are still, vegetated pockets that hold for weeks — oxbows, sloughs, backwater. Those genuinely produce.

They are also, mostly, not your property, and in many cases they are protected habitat. We will not propose treating them, and it is worth being sceptical of anyone who does. A property near a slough carries a permanently raised baseline, worst at dawn and dusk.

## What is still yours to fix

A floodplain town is not an excuse to skip the walk-round, because the on-lot sources are producing too:

- Gutters, clogged — the most common source we find anywhere
- Corrugated downspout extensions holding water in the ribs
- A tarp over the boat, trailer or woodpile
- Plant saucers, buckets, wheelbarrows, bin lids, tires
- Birdbaths nobody empties
- The low spot in the lawn that holds for a week after a storm

On a riverfront lot, add the boat cover, the canoe stored open side up, and any still pocket at the bank edge sitting out of the current.

## What a treatment buys on a floodplain lot

Honesty matters more here than anywhere else we work.

On a property that takes water, we are not eliminating production — the landscape will keep producing regardless. What a barrier treatment on the **shaded harborage** does is make your yard an uncomfortable place for adults to spend the day, so the evening is usable even while the floodplain around you is working.

That is a real improvement and a bounded one. Agreeing which of those we are buying, before we start, is the difference between a satisfied customer in August and an annoyed one.
