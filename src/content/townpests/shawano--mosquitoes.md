Shawano has more water in play than any other town we serve, and mosquito work here is mostly about being honest regarding which water we can affect.

## Three different water situations

**Shawano Lake.** Around 6,000 acres east of the city, with a shoreline of cottages, year-round homes and seasonal places. The lake itself is a poor mosquito nursery — wind and fish see to that — but the lots around it are productive in ways owners rarely notice.

**The Wolf River.** Flowing water breeds nothing. The **backwaters** are a different matter: still, vegetated pockets off the main channel that hold for weeks. Those do produce, and most of them are not yours.

**The marsh margin.** North and east of the lake there is real wetland. It produces heavily, it is protected, and it is not something anyone should be treating. On properties near it, the plan changes from elimination to management.

## What is actually breeding on a Shawano lot

Lake and river properties have their own container list, and it is not the one you would get from a suburban yard:

- The boat cover with a pocket of rain in it
- A canoe or kayak stored open side up
- The tarp over the woodpile
- Dock bumpers and float cavities
- The fish-cleaning station drain
- Cottage gutters, which on a place used two days a week nobody ever looks at
- Planters, buckets, bait containers
- Still vegetated pockets at the shoreline, out of the wind

Each of those holds enough. Some species need no more than a bottle cap, and a week of undisturbed water is the whole life cycle.

## The seasonal-property problem

A cottage used at weekends is a mosquito farm from Monday to Friday, because nobody is there to tip anything over.

The single most valuable habit on a seasonal Shawano property is doing the walk-round **when you leave**, not when you arrive. Turn the kayak over, drain the cover, empty the planters. Five days of standing water is enough time; five days of nothing is not.

We also schedule to how you actually use the place. On a seasonal property, a treatment landing three days before the weekend you are there is worth several landing on a service interval.

## Where the adults sit

Not in the open. Adult mosquitoes spend daylight in **cool shaded vegetation** — under shrubs, in dense ground cover, in the tall grass at the lot edge, on the shaded side of the cottage and outbuildings, and along the wooded margin. That band is where a barrier treatment goes.

On a lake lot the shaded margin is often between the house and the water, which is exactly the ground you want to walk across at seven in the evening. That is the part worth treating properly.

## The ceiling, stated up front

A lot backing onto marsh will never be as quiet as one in the middle of the city, and no treatment changes that. Dawn and dusk will be worse there than elsewhere regardless of what anyone applies.

We would rather say so in May than have you feel misled in August. What we can usually deliver on a Shawano lake property is a yard that is comfortable through the evening instead of one you abandon — and if that is not achievable on your specific lot, we will tell you before taking the job.
